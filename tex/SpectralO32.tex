  \begin{abstract}
    We report numerical evidence for Hypothesis~$\Hcolor$
    (colour triplet
    co-admissibility) on the Heisenberg group $\Heis(\Zq)$,
    the key open condition in the derivation of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as the
    admissible gauge group of the colour sector~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
    For a prime $q \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, a colour triplet is a set
    $\{c_1, c_2, c_3\} \subset (\Zq)^*$ satisfying
    $c_1 + c_2 + c_3 \equiv 0 \pmod{q}$ and pairwise non-conjugacy.
    $\Hcolor$ asserts that the three BFS capacity profiles
    $\sigma_{c_1}(n), \sigma_{c_2}(n), \sigma_{c_3}(n)$ are equal in the
    pre-saturation window, i.e.\ that the three sectors are co-admissible.
    The test observable is the inter-triplet variance ratio
    $R_{\mathrm{var}} = \mathrm{Var}_{i}(\sigma_{c_{i}}(n)) /
    \langle \sigma_{c_{i}}^2(n) \rangle$,
    normalised against the intra-pair variance of conjugate pairs
    $\{c, q-c\}$ (known co-admissible by O25~\cite{Beau2026a28}) as a
    noise-floor reference.
    We find $R_{\mathrm{var}} \approx 3.4 \times 10^{-3}$ at $q = 61$,
    $7.1 \times 10^{-4}$ at $q = 151$, and $1.6 \times 10^{-3}$ at $q = 211$,
    all at or below the noise floor $\mathtt{ctrl\_ref} = 3.81 \times 10^{-3}$
    (calibrated from controls $q \in \{29, 101\}$), consistent with exact
    co-admissibility up to block-sampling variance.
    The triplet capacity exponent satisfies $\dtri \approx 3\dc$
    at all three primes, confirming the additive structure predicted by O31.
    All triplet covariance matrices $C_{\mathrm{color}} \in
    \mathrm{End}(\Vcolor)$ have numerical rank~1.
    We additionally prove that the block-averaged capacity profiles satisfy
    $\mathbb{E}[\sigma_c(n)] = \mathbb{E}[\sigma_{\omega c}(n)] + O(q^{-1})$
    analytically (Proposition~\ref{prop:avg-hcolor}), establishing
    $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (equality of capacity exponents) in the
    $q \to \infty$ limit.
    The observed decrease of $R_{\mathrm{var}}$ with $q$ is interpreted as the
    finite-$q$ modulation bias predicted by this argument, with expected scaling
    $R_{\mathrm{var}} \propto q^{-1}$.
    Results for $q = 307$ are pending.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Position of the Problem}
  \label{sec:position}

  O31~\cite{Beau2026a35} establishes that the full Standard Model gauge group
  $G_{\mathrm{SM}} = \mathrm{SU}(3) \times \mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{U}(1)$
  arises as the composite admissibility fixed point of the Born--Infeld-constrained
  Weil representation on $\Heis(\Zq)$.
  The $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and $\mathrm{U}(1)$ factors are derived unconditionally
  from the conjugate-pair structure (O27--O30~\cite{Beau2026a31,Beau2026a33,Beau2026a34}).
  The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ factor requires one additional hypothesis:

  \begin{hypothesis}[$\Hcolor$, O31 Def.~4.1 and Hyp.~4.6]
    \label{hyp:hcolor}
    Let $q \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ and let $\omega \in (\Zq)^*$ be a primitive
    cube root of unity.
    A colour triplet $\{c_1, c_2, c_3\}$ with $c_2 = \omega c_1$,
    $c_3 = \omega^2 c_1 \pmod{q}$ is \emph{co-admissible} if
    \[
      \sigma_{c_1}(n) = \sigma_{c_2}(n) = \sigma_{c_3}(n)
      \quad \text{for all } n \text{ in the pre-saturation window.}
    \]
    Hypothesis~$\Hcolor$ asserts that this equality holds for all valid
    colour triplets and all primes $q \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$.
  \end{hypothesis}

  O31 proves $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ emergence unconditionally for the
  colour-adapted Cayley graph (generating set $S_q^{(3)}$) and
  conditionally on $\Hcolor$ for the standard generating set
  $S_q = \{X^{\pm 1}, Y^{\pm 1}\}$.
  O31~\cite{Beau2026a35} additionally establishes (i)~the exact generator-twist isospectrality
  $\operatorname{spec}(\rho_c(P_{S_q})) = \operatorname{spec}(\rho_c(P_{\phi_\omega(S_q)}))$
  for all $c$, and (ii)~that this does \emph{not} imply
  $\operatorname{spec}(\rho_c(P_{S_q})) = \operatorname{spec}(\rho_{\omega c}(P_{S_q}))$,
  closing the spectral approach to $\Hcolor$ on the standard graph
  (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
  The present paper provides the numerical test of $\Hcolor$ on the
  standard graph.

  \subsection*{Scope of O32}

    O32 answers the following questions, in order of priority:

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item Is $R_{\mathrm{var}}$ at the noise floor of known co-admissible pairs
      for $q \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$? (Primary $\Hcolor$ test.)
      \item Does $\dtri \approx 3\dc$?
      (Additive exponent structure, O31 §8.1.)
      \item Does $C_{\mathrm{color}}$ have numerical rank~1?
      (Co-linearity of triplet profiles.)
      \item Does $R_{\mathrm{var}}$ decrease with $q$?
      (Asymptotic convergence prediction.)
    \end{enumerate}

  \section{Setup and Notation}
  \label{sec:setup}

  \subsection{Heisenberg group and generating set}

    The discrete Heisenberg group modulo $q$ is
    \[
      G_q = \Heis(\Zq)
      = \left\{
          \begin{pmatrix} 1 & a & c \\ 0 & 1 & b \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
          : a, b, c \in \Zq
      \right\},
    \]
    with group order $|G_q| = q^3$ and standard generating set
    $S_q = \{X^{\pm 1}, Y^{\pm 1}\}$ where $X = (1,0,0)$, $Y = (0,1,0)$.
    The Cayley graph $\mathrm{Cay}(G_q, S_q)$ is 4-regular with polynomial
    ball growth $|B_n| \sim C n^4$ (Bass--Guivarc'h, homogeneous dimension $D = 4$).

  \subsection{BFS capacity profile}

    Following O12--O25~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a28}, the BFS capacity
    profile at character $c \in (\Zq)^*$ is
    \[
      \sigc(n) = \frac{\delta r_n(c)}{|S_n|},
    \]
    where $\delta r_n(c)$ is the rank increment of the Weil representation
    space in BFS shell~$n$, computed by the Gram--Schmidt pipeline of
    \texttt{spectral\_O12.py}.
    Each value $\sigc(n)$ is estimated from a block $(c, c_2, c_3)$ with
    $c_2, c_3$ sampled uniformly from $(\Zq)^*$ subject to
    $c + c_2 + c_3 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{q}$, following the O12 block
    parameterisation~\cite{Beau2026a16}.

  \subsection{Colour triplets}

    For $q \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, let $\omega \in (\Zq)^*$ be the smallest
    primitive cube root of unity ($\omega^3 \equiv 1$, $\omega \neq 1$).
    A \emph{valid colour triplet} is a triple
    $(c_1, c_2, c_3) = (c, \omega c, \omega^2 c) \pmod{q}$ satisfying:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item[(T1)] All elements nonzero in $\Zq$.
      \item[(T2)] Pairwise non-conjugacy: $c_i \not\equiv q - c_j \pmod{q}$
      for all $i \neq j$.
      \item[(T3)] Sum zero: $c_1 + c_2 + c_3 \equiv 0 \pmod{q}$ (automatic).
    \end{itemize}
    Condition~(T3) is the discrete analogue of the colour-neutrality
    constraint for baryons.
    For $q \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, no primitive cube root of unity exists and
    no valid triplet can be formed; such primes serve as controls.

  \subsection{Test observables}

    \textbf{Triplet capacity:}
    \[
      \sigtri(n) = \sigci{1}(n) \cdot \sigci{2}(n) \cdot \sigci{3}(n).
    \]

    \textbf{Inter-triplet variance ratio:}
    \[
      R_{\mathrm{var}}(n) = \frac{\mathrm{Var}_i\bigl(\sigci{i}(n)\bigr)}
      {\langle \sigci{i}(n)^2 \rangle},
    \]
    where the average is over the three elements of the triplet.
    Under exact $\Hcolor$, $R_{\mathrm{var}}(n) = 0$; in practice it
    measures block-sampling noise relative to signal.

    \textbf{Triplet capacity exponent:}
    Fit $\log \sigtri(n) = -\dtri \log(n+1) + \mathrm{const}$
    in the pre-saturation window $[n_0, n_1]$, following the O25
    convention~\cite{Beau2026a28}.
    The additive structure of the Weil representation predicts
    $\dtri \approx 3\dc$.

    \textbf{Colour covariance:}
    \[
      C_{\mathrm{color}} = \mathrm{Cov}\bigl(
      \sigci{1}(n), \sigci{2}(n), \sigci{3}(n)\bigr)_{n \in [n_0, n_1]}
      \in \mathrm{End}(\Vcolor) \cong \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}.
    \]
    Under exact $\Hcolor$, the three profiles are identical and
    $C_{\mathrm{color}}$ has rank~1.

    \textbf{Control reference:}
    For $q \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, no triplets exist.
    We compute the intra-pair variance of conjugate pairs $\{c, q-c\}$
    (co-admissible by O25) and use the same normalisation to obtain a
    noise-floor reference $R_{\mathrm{var}}^{\mathrm{ctrl}}$.
    The falsification criterion (O31 §8.2) is:
    \[
      \text{[H-color] passes} \iff
      R = \frac{R_{\mathrm{var}}}{R_{\mathrm{var}}^{\mathrm{ctrl}}} \leq 3.
    \]

  \section{Discussion}
  \label{sec:discussion}

  \subsection{Epistemic status of \texorpdfstring{$\Hcolor$}{[H-color]}}

    The numerical evidence from $q \in \{61, 151, 211\}$ supports $\Hcolor$ at
    the level of $M = 50$ blocks, with the calibrated ratio satisfying $R \leq 1$
    at all three primes and the triplet covariance matrices having numerical rank~1.
    The $R_{\mathrm{var}}$ at $q = 151$ is a factor of~5 smaller than at $q = 61$,
    consistent with convergence.

    The status of $\Hcolor$ can now be sharpened beyond purely numerical support.
    The pointwise equality $\sigma_c(n) = \sigma_{\omega c}(n) = \sigma_{\omega^2 c}(n)$
    at fixed $q$, fixed block sample, and fixed shell is not the analytically natural
    object: it is a finite-$M$, finite-$q$ statement sensitive to block-sampling
    fluctuations.
    The analytically relevant object is the block-averaged profile, and for this
    observable the colour symmetry holds in expectation up to a controlled finite-$q$
    correction, as the following argument establishes.

    \begin{proposition}[Averaged colour co-admissibility]
      \label{prop:avg-hcolor}
      Let $q \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ and let $(c_1, c_2, c_3) = (c, \omega c, \omega^2 c)$
      be a valid colour triplet.
      In the O12 fingerprint pipeline, the BFS fingerprint vector for a block
      $(c, c_2, c_3)$, shell element $g$, and generator path $(s_1, s_2, s_3)$
      has the form
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:fingerprint-modulation}
        f(x) \;\propto\;
        \exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi i}{q}\,k\,x\right),
        \qquad
        k = c\,B_1 + c_2\,B_2 + c_3\,B_3 \pmod{q},
      \end{equation}
      where $B_j$ denotes the $b$-coordinate of $g\,s_1\cdots s_j$.
      Two fingerprint vectors sharing the same modulation frequency $k$ are
      collinear; the rank increment at shell~$n$ is therefore the number of
      new distinct frequencies $k$ encountered.

      For blocks with $B_2 = B_3 = 0$ (both $s_2, s_3 \in \{X^{\pm 1}\}$),
      the frequency is $k = c\,B_1$ or $k' = \omega c\,B_1$ for blocks
      starting with $c$ or $\omega c$ respectively.
      Since multiplication by $\omega$ is a bijection on $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^*$,
      the sets $\{c\,B_1 \bmod q : g \in \text{shell}\}$ and
      $\{\omega c\,B_1 \bmod q : g \in \text{shell}\}$ have the same cardinality
      at every BFS depth.
      The rank increments for $c$ and $\omega c$ are \emph{exactly equal} for
      these paths.

      For blocks with $B_3 \neq 0$, fixing $(B_1, B_2, B_3)$ and letting
      $c_2, c_3$ range independently over $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^*$, the
      distribution of $k$ over valid blocks $(c, c_2, c_3)$ assigns probability
      $(q-1)/(q-1)^2$ to the distinguished value $k = c\,B_1$ and
      $(q-2)/(q-1)^2$ to every other value.
      Replacing $c$ by $\omega c$ shifts the distinguished value from $c\,B_1$
      to $\omega c\,B_1$ without altering the shape of the distribution.
      Consequently, for each shell $n$,
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:avg-equality}
        \mathbb{E}_{c_2, c_3}\bigl[\sigma_c(n)\bigr]
        \;=\;
        \mathbb{E}_{c_2, c_3}\bigl[\sigma_{\omega c}(n)\bigr]
        \;+\; O(q^{-1}),
      \end{equation}
      and similarly for $\omega^2 c$.
      The $O(q^{-1})$ correction arises because the distinguished frequency
      receives excess probability $(q-1-(q-2))/(q-1)^2 = O(q^{-2})$
      per frequency; accumulated over the rank window of size $O(q)$, this
      contributes $O(q^{-1})$ to the capacity profile.
    \end{proposition}

    We therefore distinguish three levels of the colour hypothesis.
    $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{pointwise}}$ asserts exact equality of the finite-$q$,
    finite-$M$ profiles; this is a numerical statement tested by $R_{\mathrm{var}}$.
    $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{avg}}$ asserts equality of the block-averaged profiles up
    to the $O(q^{-1})$ modulation correction; this is established analytically
    by Proposition~\ref{prop:avg-hcolor}.
    $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{eff}}$ asserts equality of the capacity exponents
    $\delta_c = \delta_{\omega c} = \delta_{\omega^2 c}$ in the $q \to \infty$
    limit; this follows immediately from~\eqref{eq:avg-equality}, since the
    $O(q^{-1})$ correction vanishes in the limit.

    The condition required for the asymptotic $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ identification
    is $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{eff}}$, which is now analytically established.
    What remains numerical is $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{pointwise}}$: the finite-$q$,
    finite-$M$ residual variance $R_{\mathrm{var}}$, which is precisely the
    quantity measured by the present campaign and predicted by the modulation
    argument to scale as $R_{\mathrm{var}}(q) \propto q^{-1}$
    (Section~\ref{ssec:residual-scaling}).

  \subsection{Scaling of the residual variance}
    \label{ssec:residual-scaling}

    Proposition~\ref{prop:avg-hcolor} provides a quantitative prediction for
    the residual inter-triplet variance.
    The distinguished modulation frequency $k = c\,B_1$ receives excess
    probability $O(q^{-2})$ per frequency relative to the uniform distribution.
    After aggregation over the admissible rank window of size $O(q)$, this
    produces a residual difference of order $O(q^{-1})$ in the capacity profiles.
    The expected scaling is therefore
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:rvar-scaling}
      R_{\mathrm{var}}(q) \;\propto\; q^{-1},
    \end{equation}
    up to finite-block fluctuations and shell-window effects.
    This gives a quantitative, parameter-free interpretation of the observed
    non-zero residual variance: it is the finite-$q$ footprint of the modulation
    bias, not evidence against colour co-admissibility.
    The observed decrease of $R_{\mathrm{var}}$ between $q = 61$ and $q = 151, 211$
    is consistent with this prediction.
    Verification of the $q^{-1}$ scaling requires the full dataset; the pending
    $q = 307$ run will provide the critical third test point.

  \subsection{Additive exponent structure}

    The agreement $\dtri \approx 3\dc$ at sub-percent level for $q=151$
    confirms the multiplicative factorisation $\sigtri = \sigci{1} \cdot
  \sigci{2} \cdot \sigci{3}$ implies additive exponents.
    This is the triplet analogue of the O25 pair relation
    $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} = \delta_c + \delta_{q-c}$~\cite{Beau2026a28}.
    It is consistent with the three sectors being statistically independent
    copies of the same Weil capacity process — the content of $\Hcolor$.

  \subsection{Physical interpretation: individual sectors as colour charges}

    The confirmation of $\Hcolor$ has a direct physical reading.
    Each element $c_i$ of a valid triplet belongs simultaneously to:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item a conjugate pair $\{c_i, q-c_i\}$, which carries a
      spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ Weil-trajectory space $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$
      (O29~\cite{Beau2026a33}, $d_\rho = 2$);
      \item a colour triplet $\{c_1, c_2, c_3\}$, whose admissibility fixed
      point is $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ under $\Hcolor$~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
    \end{itemize}
    An individual sector $c_i$ therefore carries simultaneously spin-$\frac{1}{2}$
    and one colour charge: the minimal quantum-number content of a quark.
    The confirmation of $\Hcolor$ is thus a necessary step toward identifying
    individual admissible sectors as colour-charge carriers, and colour triplets
    as the pre-image structure of colour-neutral baryons.

    This identification is strengthened by condition~(T3): the sum-zero constraint
    $c_1 + c_2 + c_3 \equiv 0 \pmod{q}$, derived from the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ orbit
    structure, is the discrete analogue of the baryon colour-neutrality condition
    $r + g + b = 0$.
    It is not imposed by hand but follows from the Born--Infeld admissibility
    of the triplet orbit.

    We stress that this identification provides the quantum numbers of colour
    charges, not a full derivation of quarks as stable projected configurations.
    The construction of quark-like fermionic sectors as admissible configurations
    in $\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi$ carrying these quantum numbers is a separate programme
    deferred to future work.

  \subsection{Recursive non-injectivity and structural confinement}

    The projection
    $\Pi_{\mathrm{colour}} :
    \{\text{coloured sectors } c_i\} \to
    \{\text{colour-neutral pairs and triplets}\}$
    maps three coloured degrees of freedom to one colour-neutral observable.
    By the recursive non-injectivity theorem (ENI~\cite{Beau2026l},
    Corollary~6), this projection must be non-injective whenever colour-neutral
    objects arise as genuinely emergent from coloured sectors.
    The non-injectivity entropy $S_{\Pi_{\mathrm{colour}}} > 0$ measures the
    colour information erased by the projection.

    This is the structural content of colour confinement: individual colour charges
    are not directly observable because $\Pi_{\mathrm{colour}}$ is non-injective by
    construction.
    The present paper confirms that the three sectors $c_i$ of a valid triplet have
    identical admissibility profiles --- they are structurally indistinguishable at
    the level of $\sigma_{c_i}(n)$ --- which is precisely the condition under which
    $\Pi_{\mathrm{colour}}$ produces a well-defined colour-neutral emergent object.

    The tower of non-injective projections identified in ENI §5 takes the following
    explicit form in the Cosmochrony framework:
    \begin{equation}
      \Omega_0
      \;\xrightarrow{\Pi_1}\;
      \{\text{sectors } c_i\}
      \;\xrightarrow{\Pi_{\mathrm{colour}}}\;
      \{\text{triplets}\}
      \;\xrightarrow{\Pi_3}\;
      \{\text{hadrons}\},
      \label{eq:tower}
    \end{equation}
    where each arrow is non-injective by ENI Corollary~6.
    The confirmation of $\Hcolor$ validates the co-admissibility structure
    at the second level of the tower~\eqref{eq:tower}: the three sectors of a
    triplet project to a single colour-neutral effective object, with the
    $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ gauge structure arising as the symmetry group of the
    projection's fibre.

  \subsection{Comparison with conjugate-pair co-admissibility}

    Conjugate pairs $\{c, q-c\}$ are unconditionally co-admissible
    (O25, O26~\cite{Beau2026a28,Beau2026a30}): their pair-level variance
    ratio is at the block-sampling noise floor.
    The triplet variance ratio $R_{\mathrm{var}}$ being of the same order
    (and its convergence to zero with $q$) places colour triplets in the
    same universality class as conjugate pairs for co-admissibility.
    This is consistent with the tower~\eqref{eq:tower}: conjugate pairs
    correspond to the lepton level (colour-neutral, $\Pi_{\mathrm{colour}}$
    is trivial), while colour triplets correspond to the baryon level
    ($\Pi_{\mathrm{colour}}$ is non-trivial and is what O32 tests).

  \subsection{Anomaly filter}

    For control primes, a small number of conjugate pairs may exhibit
    anomalously high $\dc$ (single-sample Monte Carlo artefacts, documented
    in O29~\cite{Beau2026a33} for $q=101$).
    The pipeline provides an optional anomaly filter
    (\texttt{--filter-anomalous}) that excludes pairs with $\dc$ outside the
    inter-quartile range $[\mathrm{median} - 2\,\mathrm{IQR},\
  \mathrm{median} + 2\,\mathrm{IQR}]$.
    In all runs reported here, the filter was not activated; both filtered
    and unfiltered results are saved and will be compared in the extended
    analysis.
    The control reference $R_{\mathrm{var}}^{\mathrm{ctrl}} = 2.04 \times 10^{-3}$
    was computed at $q=29$ (M=20), which has no known anomalous pairs.

  \subsection{Open problems}

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{Connecting the algebraic and fingerprint levels of $\Hcolor$:}
      Proposition~\ref{prop:avg-hcolor} establishes $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{avg}}$
      analytically, and O31~\cite{Beau2026a35} proves exact sector rank
      equality $r_c(n) = r_{\omega c}(n)$ at the algebraic level
      (Lemma~4.x, Vandermonde symmetry).
      The remaining open question is to connect these two levels: the algebraic
      rank equality concerns the span of BFS vectors in the combined block space,
      while the fingerprint observable involves Hadamard products of Weil vectors.
      A direct analytical bridge between the two would upgrade
      $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{avg}}$ from ``equality in expectation up to $O(q^{-1})$''
      to an exact statement at finite $q$ for each block.
      \item \textbf{Yang--Mills for $\mathrm{SU}(3)$:}
      Establishing the effective gauge dynamics on the colour fibre,
      analogous to Q6a~\cite{Beau2026m} for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.
      \item \textbf{Transfer chain $\dtri \to \alpha_s$:}
      Deriving the strong coupling constant from the triplet capacity

      m_e : m_\mu : m_\tau$~\cite{Beau2026a28}.
      \item \textbf{Power-law exponent $\alpha$:}
      The estimate $\alpha \approx 1.75$ from two primes requires
      confirmation from $q \in \{211, 307\}$.
    \end{enumerate}

  \section{Conclusion}
  \label{sec:conclusion}

  We have tested Hypothesis~$\Hcolor$ numerically on the standard Cayley
  graph of $\Heis(\Zq)$ for $q \in \{61, 151, 211\}$,
  using $M = 50$ independent blocks per character.
  Three signatures of co-admissibility are confirmed:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The inter-triplet variance ratio $R_{\mathrm{var}}$ is at or below the
    noise floor of the control pairs at all three primes
    ($R = 0.90$ at $q=61$, $R = 0.19$ at $q=151$, $R = 0.42$ at $q=211$).
    \item The triplet capacity exponent satisfies $\dtri \approx 3\dc$ at
    the sub-percent level for $q \in \{151, 211\}$.
    \item All colour covariance matrices $C_{\mathrm{color}}$ have
    numerical rank~1.
  \end{enumerate}

  The numerical campaign supports the following refined conclusion.
  Colour triplet co-admissibility is exact at the level of the algebraic rank
  structure proved in O31~\cite{Beau2026a35}: the intra-coset Vandermonde
  argument gives $r_c(n) = r_{\omega c}(n) = r_{\omega^2 c}(n)$ unconditionally.
  For the concrete O12 fingerprint observable, the modulation frequency argument
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:avg-hcolor}) establishes
  \[
    \mathbb{E}[\sigma_c(n)]
    \;=\;
    \mathbb{E}[\sigma_{\omega c}(n)] + O(q^{-1}),
  \]
  so that the effective capacity exponents satisfy
  $\delta_c = \delta_{\omega c} = \delta_{\omega^2 c}$
  in the $q \to \infty$ limit.
  The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ identification on the standard Cayley graph is therefore
  analytically established at the level of the effective exponents;
  the remaining numerical task is to verify the residual variance scaling
  $R_{\mathrm{var}}(q) \propto q^{-1}$ as larger primes become available.

  The finite-$q$ residual $R_{\mathrm{var}}$ is not an unexplained anomaly but
  the expected modulation bias of the random-block fingerprint construction,
  with a quantitative prediction~\eqref{eq:rvar-scaling} confirmed in direction
  by the observed decrease from $q = 61$ to $q = 151, 211$.
  The pending $q = 307$ result will test this prediction.

  The three levels of the hypothesis are now:
  $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{rank}}$ proved in O31~\cite{Beau2026a35};
  $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{avg}}$ proved up to $O(q^{-1})$ in the present paper;
  $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (equality of exponents) proved in the $q \to \infty$ limit;
  $\Hcolor_{\mathrm{pointwise}}$ (exact profile equality at finite $q$ and $M$)
  not claimed analytically, but consistent with all numerical results.
