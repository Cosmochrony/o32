  \begin{abstract}
    Version~2.0 replaces version~1.5 and invalidates its interpretation.
    Version~1.5 presented an inter-triplet variance ratio $\Rvar$, the slope relation
    $\dtri\approx3\delta_c$ and the numerical rank of a $3\times3$ covariance as a test of
    hypothesis~$\Hcolor$, and read the outcome as support for an $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ colour structure.
    We show that this diagnostic cannot test~$\Hcolor$.
    The O12 three-character capacity profile is exactly invariant under multiplication of the
    whole character block by any unit $\lambda$ of $\Zq$: each fingerprint vector is a single
    Fourier character, rank increments count distinct frequencies, and $\lambda$ permutes them.
    Under the sampling law of the campaign, the profile attached to a character $c$ therefore has
    the same distribution for every $c\neq0$, with no correction term and no role for cube roots
    of unity.
    The three version-1.5 observables consequently have laws independent of the characters
    tested: colour triplets, conjugate pairs and arbitrary triples are indistinguishable, as null
    controls at $q=61$ and $q=151$ confirm.
    The relation $\dtri\approx3\delta_c$ is an identity of least-squares fitting, and the
    rank-one covariance reflects identically distributed profiles.
    The stored campaign is reproduced exactly; rebuilt on shells unaffected by rank saturation,
    the calibration gives comparable ratios for all three kinds of character sets.
    Version~1.5's Proposition~1, the pointwise and rank levels imported from O31, and the
    $\mathrm{SU}(3)$, quark, baryon, confinement and $\beta^{*}$ readings are withdrawn.
    Hypothesis~$\Hcolor$ is neither confirmed nor refuted: it is not testable with this observable.
  \end{abstract}

  \paragraph{Keywords.}
    Heisenberg group, Weil representation, BFS capacity, null control, unit scaling,
    diagnostic limits, admissibility sub-programme.

  \section{Status of this version}
  \label{sec:status}

  Version~1.5 of this record~\cite{Beau2026a36} reported a numerical campaign on
  $\Heis(\Zq)$ for $q\in\{61,151,211,307\}$, with controls $q\in\{29,101\}$, and presented
  it as a test of hypothesis~$\Hcolor$ (colour-triplet co-admissibility) in the sense of
  O31~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
  O31 has since been replaced by a withdrawal notice: its colour-triplet co-admissibility,
  arithmetic criterion and every gauge-group derivation are withdrawn.
  The present version does not repair the version-1.5 conclusions.
  It explains why the diagnostic used there could not have tested~$\Hcolor$, and makes that
  explanation its central result.

  \paragraph{Withdrawn from version~1.5.}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The claim that the campaign tests, confirms or numerically supports~$\Hcolor$, and the
    verdict ``$\Hcolor$ passes'' attached to the criterion $R\leq3$.
    \item Proposition~1 of version~1.5 (averaged colour co-admissibility up to $O(q^{-1})$) and
    the prediction $\Rvar\propto q^{-1}$.
    The exact statement is Corollary~\ref{cor:law} below; it holds for every unit, carries no
    colour content, and has no correction term.
    \item The pointwise and rank levels of~$\Hcolor$ attributed to O31, and every statement that
    the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ identification on the standard Cayley graph is analytically established.
    \item The additive-structure reading of $\dtri\approx3\delta_c$ as a prediction confirmed by
    the data (Proposition~\ref{prop:ols}).
    \item The evidential weight given to the numerical rank of $\Ccol$ (Remark~\ref{rmk:ccol}).
    \item The physical interpretation: individual sectors as colour charges, quark quantum
    numbers, colour triplets as baryon pre-images, structural confinement and the tower of
    non-injective projections.
    \item The open problem ``transfer chain $\dtri\to\alpha_s$'' as an analogue of
    $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\to\beta^{*}\to m_e:m_\mu:m_\tau$.
    O6 derives only the inequality $\beta\leq1/(\alpha+\tfrac12)$ and gives a countermodel to
    equality~\cite{Beau2026a10}, so no such chain is available.
    Version~1.5 also cited O24 where O25~\cite{Beau2026a29} was meant; that citation is
    corrected.
    \item The calibration values $\mathtt{ctrl\_ref}\in\{3.81,\,1.657,\,2.04\}\times10^{-3}$ and the
    ratios $R$ derived from them (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}).
  \end{enumerate}

  \paragraph{What replaces them.}
  An exact invariance theorem for the O12 profile (Section~\ref{sec:invariance}), its
  consequence for the law of the campaign observables, null controls, an exact reproduction of
  the stored campaign, and a calibration rebuilt on usable shells
  (Sections~\ref{sec:numerics}--\ref{sec:calibration}).
  Earlier versions remain available in the record's version history for traceability.

  \section{Setup}
  \label{sec:setup}

  \subsection{Group, generators and BFS shells}

    Let $q$ be an odd prime and $G_q=\Heis(\Zq)$, with elements $(a,b,z)$ and product
    $(a,b,z)(a',b',z')=(a+a',\,b+b',\,z+z'+ab')$.
    The generating set is $S_q=\{X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}\}$ with $X=(1,0,0)$ and $Y=(0,1,0)$.
    The BFS shells $S_n$ of the Cayley graph from the identity are those of
    O12~\cite{Beau2026a16}; $B_n=S_0\cup\dots\cup S_n$ denotes the ball of depth~$n$.
    The second coordinate is additive, $b(gs)=b(g)+b(s)$, with $b(s)\in\{0,\pm1\}$ on~$S_q$.

  \subsection{Fingerprints and the capacity profile}

    For $c\in\Zqs$ write $\psi_c(t)=e^{2\pi i ct/q}$ and let $\rho_c$ act on $\mathbb{C}^q$ by
    \begin{equation}
      \bigl(\rho_c(a,b,z)f\bigr)(x)=\psi_c\bigl(z+b(x-a)\bigr)f(x-a).
      \label{eq:schrodinger}
    \end{equation}
    A \emph{block} is a triple $\mathbf c=(c_1,c_2,c_3)\in(\Zqs)^3$ with total
    $c=c_1+c_2+c_3\neq0$.
    For a shell element $g$ and generators $s_1,s_2,s_3\in S_q$, put $g_1=gs_1$,
    $g_2=gs_1s_2$, $g_3=gs_1s_2s_3$, and let $u_0=q^{-1/2}(1,\dots,1)$.
    The O12 fingerprint vector is
    \begin{equation}
      f_{\mathbf c}(g;s_1,s_2,s_3)
      =\bigl(\rho_{c_1}(g_1)u_0\bigr)\odot\bigl(\rho_{c_2}(g_2)u_0\bigr)\odot
      \bigl(\rho_{c_3}(g_3)u_0\bigr),
      \label{eq:fingerprint}
    \end{equation}
    with $\odot$ the coordinatewise product.
    Let $r_n(\mathbf c)$ be the dimension of the span of all fingerprint vectors with $g\in B_n$,
    computed by Gram--Schmidt in shell order, and $\Delta r_n=r_n-r_{n-1}$.
    The capacity profile is
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma_{\mathbf c}(n)=\frac{\Delta r_n(\mathbf c)}{|S_n|}.
      \label{eq:sigma}
    \end{equation}

  \subsection{The sampling law of the campaign}

    For a character $c_1\in\Zqs$, the version-1.5 campaign draws $c_2,c_3$ independently and
    uniformly from $\Zqs$, rejecting draws with $c_1+c_2+c_3=0$.
    Write $\mathcal L_{c_1}$ for the resulting law of the block $\mathbf c=(c_1,c_2,c_3)$.
    The profile assigned to $c_1$ is the mean $\bar\sigma_{c_1}$ of $M=50$ independent profiles
    $\sigma_{\mathbf c}$ with $\mathbf c\sim\mathcal L_{c_1}$, each character using its own
    random stream.
    A \emph{colour triplet} in version~1.5 is an orbit $(c,\omega c,\omega^2c)$ with $\omega$ a
    primitive cube root of unity, $q\equiv1\pmod3$, and no two members conjugate
    ($c_i\neq-c_j$); its members sum to zero because $1+\omega+\omega^2=0$.
    The version-1.5 abstract used a different definition (sum zero plus non-conjugacy); the
    campaign used the orbit definition.

  \section{Exact unit-scaling invariance}
  \label{sec:invariance}

  \begin{lemma}[Single-frequency fingerprints]
    \label{lem:single}
    For every block, shell element and generator path,
    \[
      f_{\mathbf c}(g;s_1,s_2,s_3)=q^{-3/2}\,e^{2\pi i A/q}\,e_B,
      \qquad e_B(x)=\psi_1(Bx),
      \qquad B=c_1b(g_1)+c_2b(g_2)+c_3b(g_3),
    \]
    for some $A\in\Zq$ depending on the block, $g$ and the path.
  \end{lemma}

  \begin{proof}
    By~\eqref{eq:schrodinger}, $\bigl(\rho_c(a,b,z)u_0\bigr)(x)=q^{-1/2}\psi_c(z-ab)\,\psi_c(bx)$.
    Multiplying the three factors in~\eqref{eq:fingerprint} gives the phase
    $\sum_i c_i\bigl(z(g_i)-a(g_i)b(g_i)\bigr)$ and the frequency $\sum_ic_ib(g_i)$.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{lemma}[Rank counts frequencies]
    \label{lem:count}
    Let $K_n(\mathbf c)\subset\Zq$ be the set of frequencies $B$ realised by $g\in B_n$ and all
    paths. Then $r_n(\mathbf c)=|K_n(\mathbf c)|$.
  \end{lemma}

  \begin{proof}
    The characters $e_B$, $B\in\Zq$, are pairwise orthogonal.
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:single} every fingerprint is a non-zero multiple of one of them, so the
    span of a family of fingerprints is the span of the distinct $e_B$ it contains.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[Closed form]
    \label{rmk:closed}
    O33~\cite[Theorem~1]{Beau2026a37} gives
    $K_n(\mathbf c)=c[-(n+1),n+1]+\{0,\pm(c_2+c_3)\}+\{0,\pm c_3\}$ in $\Zq$, at every depth at
    which the BFS ball $B_n$ is complete.
    Only the second Heisenberg coordinate enters $K_n$; the group enters $\sigma$ through the
    shell sizes $|S_n|$ alone.
    The profile depends on the block only through $\bigl((c_2+c_3)/c,\;c_3/c\bigr)$.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{theorem}[Unit-scaling invariance]
    \label{thm:invariance}
    For every block $\mathbf c$, every $\lambda\in\Zqs$ and every $n$,
    \[
      \sigma_{\lambda\mathbf c}(n)=\sigma_{\mathbf c}(n),
      \qquad \lambda\mathbf c=(\lambda c_1,\lambda c_2,\lambda c_3).
    \]
  \end{theorem}

  \begin{proof}
    The frequency of Lemma~\ref{lem:single} is linear in the block, so
    $K_n(\lambda\mathbf c)=\lambda K_n(\mathbf c)$.
    Multiplication by a unit is a bijection of $\Zq$, hence
    $r_n(\lambda\mathbf c)=r_n(\mathbf c)$ for all $n$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:count}, and the
    shell sizes do not depend on the block.
  \end{proof}

  The invariance holds for every unit.
  The case $\lambda=\omega$ is the version-1.5 colour orbit, the case $\lambda=-1$ the
  conjugate pair $\{c,q-c\}$; neither is distinguished from any other unit.
  Multiplying the first character alone is not a symmetry: matched blocks
  $(c,c_2,c_3)$ and $(\omega c,c_2,c_3)$ have different profiles in general.

  \begin{corollary}[Character-independent law]
    \label{cor:law}
    For all $c_1,c_1'\in\Zqs$, the profile $\sigma_{\mathbf c}$ has the same distribution under
    $\mathcal L_{c_1}$ and under $\mathcal L_{c_1'}$.
    In particular $\mathbb E_{\mathcal L_{c_1}}[\sigma(n)]=\mathbb E_{\mathcal L_{c_1'}}[\sigma(n)]$
    exactly, for every $n$.
  \end{corollary}

  \begin{proof}
    Let $\lambda=c_1'/c_1$.
    The map $(c_2,c_3)\mapsto(\lambda c_2,\lambda c_3)$ is a bijection of $(\Zqs)^2$ that maps
    the rejected set $\{c_1+c_2+c_3=0\}$ onto $\{c_1'+c_2+c_3=0\}$, so it pushes
    $\mathcal L_{c_1}$ forward to $\mathcal L_{c_1'}$.
    Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance} identifies the profiles of corresponding blocks.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{corollary}[No observable of the campaign can separate character sets]
    \label{cor:observables}
    Let $\{c^{(1)},\dots,c^{(k)}\}$ be distinct characters whose mean profiles are computed from
    independent draws.
    Then the mean profiles $\bar\sigma_{c^{(1)}},\dots,\bar\sigma_{c^{(k)}}$ are independent and
    identically distributed, with a law that does not depend on the characters.
    Every statistic of these profiles, in particular $\Rvar$, $\dtri$ and $\Ccol$, has the same law
    for a colour triplet, for a conjugate pair with any third character, and for any other
    triple.
  \end{corollary}

  \begin{proof}
    Independence holds by construction; identical distribution is Corollary~\ref{cor:law}
    applied to each mean of $M$ independent profiles.
  \end{proof}

  Corollary~\ref{cor:observables} is the reason the version-1.5 criterion always passed:
  $\Rvar$ measures the finite-$M$ sampling dispersion of identically distributed means.
  It cannot fail for colour triplets more often than for any other choice of characters.
  The corresponding pseudo-random streams are independent in the statistical sense used by the
  campaign; the null controls of Section~\ref{sec:numerics} check the consequence directly.

  \begin{proposition}[Slope additivity is an identity]
    \label{prop:ols}
    Fit $\log y(n)=-\delta\log(n+1)+\mathrm{const}$ by ordinary least squares on a common set of
    shells $N$ with all values positive.
    If $\sigma_{\mathrm{tri}}=\bar\sigma_{c_1}\bar\sigma_{c_2}\bar\sigma_{c_3}$, then
    $\dtri=\delta(\bar\sigma_{c_1})+\delta(\bar\sigma_{c_2})+\delta(\bar\sigma_{c_3})$.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
    $\log\sigma_{\mathrm{tri}}=\sum_i\log\bar\sigma_{c_i}$, and the least-squares slope on a fixed
    design is a linear function of the response vector.
  \end{proof}

  The deviations of 0.7--2.2\% between $\dtri$ and $3\bar\delta_c$ reported in version~1.5 do
  not test any structure.
  There, $\bar\delta_c$ was a mean of per-block fits over twelve characters, whereas $\dtri$
  fitted the product of three mean profiles, on windows that at $q=61$ held one or two shells
  (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}).

  \begin{remark}[The covariance $\Ccol$]
    \label{rmk:ccol}
    $\Ccol$ is the $3\times3$ covariance, over shells, of three identically distributed mean
    profiles $\bar\sigma_{c_i}(n)=\mu(n)+\varepsilon_i(n)$, with $\mu$ the common expectation.
    Its leading eigenvalue is the variance of the decaying function $\mu$ over the window, and the
    other two are of the order of the sampling noise.
    Numerical rank one is therefore expected for every triple of characters, and the matrix carries
    no representation-theoretic content: no space $\tilde V_{\mathrm{color}}$ is constructed on
    which it would act.
  \end{remark}

  \section{Exact checks and null controls}
  \label{sec:numerics}

  All checks use the version-1.5 code in \texttt{code/} and the null-control and rebuild scripts
  added in this version in \texttt{code/v2/}.
  The frequency-counting profile \texttt{sigma\_freq.py} implements Lemma~\ref{lem:count}.

  \begin{itemize}
    \item[(E1)] \emph{Frequency counting versus Gram--Schmidt.}
    On three random blocks at each of $q=61$ and $q=151$ the two profiles agree exactly (maximum
    difference~$0$); the same agreement underlies the exact reproduction of
    Section~\ref{sec:calibration}, which used frequency counting throughout.
    \item[(E2)] \emph{Unit scaling.}
    For thirty random blocks and thirty random units $\lambda$ that are not cube roots of unity,
    $\sigma_{\lambda\mathbf c}=\sigma_{\mathbf c}$ at every shell, at $q=61$ and $q=151$ (maximum
    difference~$0$).
    \item[(E3)] \emph{Null control.}
    With the campaign protocol ($M=50$, version-1.5 windows), the four version-1.5 colour triplets
    and twenty arbitrary triples containing no cube-root orbit give the window-averaged ratios
    below, in units of $10^{-3}$ (the version-1.5 normalisation, population variance).
  \end{itemize}

  \section{Reproduction and rebuilt calibration}
  \label{sec:calibration}

  \subsection{Reproduction of the stored campaign}

    The stored arrays \texttt{results\_o32.npz} and \texttt{results\_o32\_controls.npz} are
    reproduced exactly (maximum difference~$0$) with $M=50$, seed~$42$ and the per-character
    streams of \texttt{o32\_triplet\_pipeline.py}.
    Two conventions coexist in them.
    The test-prime means are truncated to the shortest of the $M$ block profiles, whereas the
    control means are zero-padded to the longest.
    The version-1.5 ratio $R$ compared quantities computed under the two conventions.
    Version~1.5 also stated three different noise floors ($3.81$, $1.657$ and
    $2.04\times10^{-3}$); the first two depend on which primes were summarised together, and the
    third matches no stored run.
    The parameter table gave $M=20$ for $q=29$; the stored control used $M=50$.

  \subsection{Usable shells}

    A shell $n\geq1$ is \emph{usable} for a set of characters if no block contributing to any of
    their profiles has reached rank $q$ by shell $n$.
    On usable shells no increment is truncated by saturation and no mean includes padding.
    Usable shells are few at small primes: none at $q=29$, where every block reaches rank $q$ at $n=1$, one
    or two at $q=61$, four at $q=101$, six to eight at $q=151$, ten to twelve at $q=211$ and fifteen to
    nineteen at $q=307$ (Table~\ref{tab:rebuilt}).
    The windows of the stored version-1.5 runs ($[2,3]$ for $q=29$, although the paper's table gave $[2,5]$;
    $[2,8]$ for $q=61$) therefore lay mostly beyond saturation, and the version-1.5 value of $\Rvar$ at $q=61$
    was the single shell $n=2$.

  \subsection{Rebuilt ratios}

    On usable shells only, define
    \[
      \Rvar=\Bigl\langle
      \frac{\operatorname{Var}_i\bigl(\bar\sigma_{c_i}(n)\bigr)}{\langle\bar\sigma_{c_i}(n)^2\rangle_i}
      \Bigr\rangle_{n\ \mathrm{usable}},
    \]
    with the unbiased variance, so that sets of two and three characters are comparable.
    For each prime the table gives the mean over groups and, in parentheses, the range over groups.
    Colour triplets are the four version-1.5 triplets; conjugate pairs are the four smallest pairs
    $\{c,q-c\}$; arbitrary triples are twelve random triples containing no cube-root orbit, drawn with seed
    $20261119+q$.
    At test primes the pairs and triples use fresh streams, so their values depend on the seed; at control
    primes the pairs are the version-1.5 control characters.

  \section{What remains open}
  \label{sec:open}

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{A discriminating observable.}
    Any test of a colour orbit must use an observable that is not invariant under all units, since
    Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance} makes the O12 profile blind to the choice of the character up to a
    common scale.
    No such observable is proposed here.
    \item \emph{The status of~$\Hcolor$.}
    The hypothesis is neither confirmed nor refuted by the present record.
    Its formulation, the selection of an order-three orbit, and any group identification depend on
    a carrier and a theorem that the programme does not currently supply~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
    \item \emph{Physical readings.}
    No colour charge, quark, baryon or confinement statement is supported by this record.
  \end{enumerate}

  \section{Conclusion}
  \label{sec:conclusion}

  The O12 three-character capacity profile is exactly invariant under scaling of the whole block
  by any unit of $\Zq$.
  Under the version-1.5 sampling law, every character therefore carries the same distribution of
  profiles, and the inter-triplet variance ratio, the slope relation and the covariance rank have
  laws that do not depend on which characters are compared.
  The version-1.5 campaign, reproduced exactly here, measured sampling dispersion, not colour
  co-admissibility.
  Its verdict and its physical interpretation are withdrawn; the diagnostic's limits, now proved,
  are this record's result.
